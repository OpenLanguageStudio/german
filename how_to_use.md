# How to use

The coursebook is organised by level and then by category. Work through a level's concepts in order, since each one builds on the last, and use its grammar articles as a reference alongside them. Writing and vocabulary articles stand alone and can be read in any order.

## How an article is laid out

Every article follows the same order. A short lead paragraph says what the topic is. The teaching sections then explain it one step at a time, each with a few example sentences and their translations. *Hear it in practice* collects around twenty sentences that use the topic. The *Summary* lists the key points and the most common mistakes, and *Related articles* links to other pages on the same subject.

## The marks in examples

Some words in an example sentence are marked to show the point being taught. A word with a coloured dotted underline is marked by its part of speech, such as a verb, a noun or a preposition. A shaded phrase is marked by its role in the sentence, such as the dative case. In *Ich gebe {dativ}`meiner Schwester` ein Buch*, the shading shows that *meiner Schwester* is in the dative. Only the words the section is about are marked, never the whole sentence.

## The audio tracks

The sentences under *Hear it in practice* are written to be heard. On this site they are shown with their translations hidden, behind a button that reveals them. The same sentences are recorded as an audio track in the Open Language app, in the same order, so a learner can listen to an article's examples away from the page.
