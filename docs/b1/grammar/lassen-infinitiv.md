# lassen + Infinitiv

*lassen* combined with a bare infinitive is one of the most flexible structures in German. With a single verb, you can say that you are having something done by someone else, that you are letting someone do something, or that something is (im)possible. All three uses share one important rule: the infinitive that follows **never** takes *zu*.

| | Präsens | Präteritum |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **lasse** | ließ |
| du | **lässt** | ließt |
| er / sie / es | **lässt** | ließ |
| wir | **lassen** | ließen |
| ihr | **lasst** | ließt |
| sie / Sie | **lassen** | ließen |

---

## Causative: having something done

You use the causative when you are not doing an action yourself but arranging for someone else to do it. Think of it as outsourcing, to a repair shop, a professional, or anyone else.

> {verb}`lassen` (conjugated) + object + infinitive

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich **lasse** das Auto {verb}`reparieren`.
: I'm having the car repaired.

Sie **lässt** ihre Wohnung {verb}`renovieren`.
: She's having her flat renovated.

Wir **lassen** die Fenster {verb}`putzen`.
: We're having the windows cleaned.

Er **lässt** ein Paket {verb}`liefern`.
: He's having a package delivered.

Ich **lasse** mir den Arm {verb}`untersuchen`.
: I'm having my arm examined.
:::

:::{tip}
Notice the last example: *Ich lasse **mir** den Arm untersuchen.* The dative *mir* shows that the action is done **to me** (by a doctor). This is a common pattern when referring to parts of the body or personal services.
:::

---

## Permissive: letting someone do something

When you allow or permit someone else to do something, the structure looks exactly the same, but the focus shifts to the **person** you are giving permission to.

> {verb}`lassen` (conjugated) + person + infinitive

:::{div} ol-examples
Die Lehrerin **lässt** die Kinder früher {verb}`gehen`.
: The teacher lets the children leave early.

Meine Eltern **lassen** mich allein {verb}`reisen`.
: My parents let me travel alone.

Der Vermieter **lässt** keine Haustiere {verb}`rein`.
: The landlord doesn't allow pets inside.

Er **lässt** seinen jüngeren Bruder das Auto {verb}`fahren`.
: He lets his younger brother drive the car.

**Lasst** uns nach Hause {verb}`gehen`!
: Let's go home!
:::

:::{note}
The causative and permissive uses share the same structure. Context always makes the meaning clear. *Sie lässt die Kinder malen* could theoretically mean "she's having the children paint (something)" or "she lets the children paint", in practice, you would understand from the situation which meaning applies.

Also notice the last example: *lass uns / lasst uns* + infinitive is the standard German equivalent of "let's …". *Lass uns essen!* (Let's eat!), very common in everyday speech.
:::

## Possibility: sich lassen + Infinitiv

*Sich lassen + Infinitiv* is a neat alternative to the passive voice. It expresses whether something **can** or **cannot** be done, without naming who does it. It often carries a practical, evaluative tone.

> subject + {verb}`lässt` + {pronoun}`sich` + infinitive

This is equivalent to *kann + Partizip II werden* (can be done):

| sich lassen | Passive equivalent |
|---|---|
| Das lässt sich machen. | Das kann gemacht werden. |
| Das lässt sich nicht ändern. | Das kann nicht geändert werden. |

:::{div} ol-examples
Das **lässt sich** leicht {verb}`erklären`.
: That can easily be explained.

Die Tür **lässt sich** nicht {verb}`öffnen`.
: The door can't be opened.

Das **lässt sich** nicht so einfach {verb}`lösen`.
: That can't be solved so easily.

Dieser Wein **lässt sich** gut {verb}`trinken`.
: This wine drinks well. / This wine is easy to drink.

Das **lässt sich** {verb}`machen`!
: That can be done! / That's doable!
:::

:::{tip}
*Das lässt sich machen* is a very natural, everyday phrase meaning "That's doable" or "We can sort that out". It sounds more fluent and confident than *Das kann gemacht werden*, and you will hear it frequently in conversation and at work.
:::

## Perfekt with lassen

In the Perfekt, *lassen* behaves exactly like a modal verb. When it is combined with a following infinitive, it forms a **double infinitive** at the end of the sentence. The other verb's infinitive comes first, and *lassen* follows it in last position.

> **haben** (conjugated) + ... + infinitive + **lassen**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich habe das Auto reparieren **lassen**.
: I had the car repaired.

Sie hat die Kinder draußen spielen **lassen**.
: She let the children play outside.

Wir haben die Wohnung streichen **lassen**.
: We had the flat painted.

Das hat sich leider nicht ändern **lassen**.
: Unfortunately, that couldn't be changed.

Hast du dich endlich beim Arzt untersuchen **lassen**?
: Did you finally get yourself checked out by a doctor?
:::

:::{warning}
The most common mistake is using *gelassen* instead of *lassen* in the double infinitive:

- Incorrect: Ich habe das Auto reparieren ~~gelassen~~.
- Correct: Ich habe das Auto reparieren **lassen**.

Use *gelassen* only when *lassen* is the main verb with no following infinitive and it means "to leave (something behind)":

- Ich habe den Schlüssel zu Hause **gelassen**. _(I left the key at home.)_
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note
- *lassen* takes a **bare infinitive**, never use *zu* after it
- Three main uses: **causative** (having something done), **permissive** (letting someone do something), *sich lassen* (expressing possibility)
- **Conjugation note:** the verb has an umlaut change in the second and third person singular – *du **lässt***, *er/sie/es **lässt***
- **Perfekt:** like modal verbs, lassen uses a **double infinitive** – *hat ... lassen*, **not** *hat ... gelassen*
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution
- *Ich lasse das Auto ~~zu reparieren~~.* → Ich lasse das Auto **reparieren**. (bare infinitive, never use *zu* with lassen)
- *Sie hat die Kinder spielen ~~gelassen~~.* → Sie hat die Kinder spielen **lassen**. (double infinitive in Perfekt, use *lassen*, not *gelassen*)
- *Das ~~lässt sich erklären werden~~.* → Das **lässt sich erklären**. (*sich lassen* already replaces the *kann … werden* passive, do not add *werden*)
- *Er ~~lässt~~ sein Buch hier.* → Er **lässt** sein Buch hier. ✓ But Perfekt: Er hat sein Buch hier **gelassen**. (*gelassen* is correct here because there is no following infinitive and the meaning is "to leave behind")
:::
