# Passiv und Passiversatzformen

At B2, forming the passive correctly is only half the job. The other half is choosing the most natural structure for the context and register. This page shows how to build the main passive tenses and when to switch to common *Passiversatzformen* like *sein + zu + Infinitiv*, *sich lassen + Infinitiv*, and adjectives ending in *-bar* or *-lich*.

*Examples:*
- *The report is being reviewed at the moment.*
- *The issue had already been clarified before the meeting started.*
- *This can be solved quickly.*

---

## Passive baseline: what the passive does

The passive puts the process or result in focus, not the person doing it. At B2, this is especially useful in formal writing, reports, and process descriptions where the agent is unknown, obvious, or less important.

| Focus | Typical form | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Process | **werden** + Partizip II | action in progress or action as event |
| Agent optional | von + Dativ | add who did it only when relevant |
| Formal style | passive common | objective, impersonal tone |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der Vertrag **wird** aktuell **geprüft**.
: The contract is currently being reviewed.

Die Ergebnisse **wurden** gestern **veröffentlicht**.
: The results were published yesterday.

Die Entscheidung **wurde von** der Kommission **getroffen**.
: The decision was made by the commission.

In der Abteilung **wird** zurzeit viel **diskutiert**.
: There is a lot of discussion in the department at the moment.
:::

:::{note}
At B2, you should actively choose between active and passive for text flow. Passive is helpful when the topic is the process or affected object, but overusing it can make a text heavy.
:::

## Bildung: Präsens und Präteritum

These two forms are the foundation of the *Vorgangspassiv*. Use *Präsens* for current or general processes and *Präteritum* mainly for written past narration.

| Tense | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Präsens | **werden** (present) + Partizip II | Die Daten **werden analysiert**. |
| Präteritum | **wurden/wurde** + Partizip II | Die Daten **wurden analysiert**. |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Die Unterlagen **werden** heute noch **gescannt**.
: The documents are still being scanned today.

Neue Mitarbeitende **werden** im ersten Monat intensiv **eingearbeitet**.
: New employees are trained intensively in the first month.

Das Produkt **wurde** im letzten Jahr komplett **überarbeitet**.
: The product was completely revised last year.

Die Bewerbungen **wurden** nach klaren Kriterien **bewertet**.
: The applications were evaluated according to clear criteria.
:::

:::{tip}
In everyday spoken German, many people prefer *Perfekt* for past events. In formal reports and written summaries, *Präteritum* passive remains very common.
:::

---

## Bildung: Perfekt und Plusquamperfekt

For compound passive tenses, German uses *sein* as the auxiliary. The typical B2 challenge is placing *worden* correctly and avoiding confusion with *geworden*.

| Tense | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Perfekt | **sein** (present) + Partizip II + **worden** | Der Plan **ist angepasst worden**. |
| Plusquamperfekt | **sein** (präteritum) + Partizip II + **worden** | Der Plan **war angepasst worden**. |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Die Rechnung **ist** bereits **bezahlt worden**.
: The invoice has already been paid.

Mehrere Fehler **sind** rechtzeitig **entdeckt worden**.
: Several errors were discovered in time.

Der Vertrag **war** vor dem Termin **unterschrieben worden**.
: The contract had been signed before the appointment.

Das Problem **war** intern schon mehrfach **angesprochen worden**.
: The problem had already been raised internally several times.
:::

:::{warning}
In passive perfect forms, use **worden**, not *geworden*.
- Correct: Das Paket ist geliefert **worden**.
- Wrong: Das Paket ist geliefert *geworden*.
:::

## Bildung: Passiv mit Modalverben

With modal verbs, passive meaning and modality combine in one clause. At B2, the key is handling the verb-final cluster accurately in longer sentences.

> Modalverb (conjugated) + Partizip II + **werden**

| Meaning | Typical modal passive | Example |
|---|---|---|
| necessity | muss ... werden | Der Bericht **muss abgeschlossen werden**. |
| possibility | kann ... werden | Die Datei **kann geteilt werden**. |
| recommendation/expectation | sollte ... werden | Der Fehler **sollte dokumentiert werden**. |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Die Ergebnisse **müssen** bis Freitag **ausgewertet werden**.
: The results must be evaluated by Friday.

Der Antrag **kann** online **eingereicht werden**.
: The application can be submitted online.

In sensiblen Fällen **sollte** zuerst intern **geklärt werden**.
: In sensitive cases, things should first be clarified internally.

Der Termin **musste** kurzfristig **verschoben werden**.
: The appointment had to be postponed at short notice.
:::

:::{tip}
Long modal-passive clauses are common in formal German. Keep the final block intact: Partizip II + werden.
:::

## Passiversatzformen: overview and contrast

German often avoids full passive forms when a shorter active-looking structure sounds more idiomatic. At B2, you should recognize the nuance: all options can be "passive-like", but their tone is not identical.

| Structure | Core meaning | Typical tone |
|---|---|---|
| werden-Passiv | process/event focus | neutral to formal |
| sein + zu + Infinitiv | necessity/possibility | formal, written |
| sich lassen + Infinitiv | practical possibility | neutral, frequent |
| sein + -bar/-lich adjective | inherent quality | compact, evaluative |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der Bericht **wird morgen erstellt**.
: The report is being prepared tomorrow.

Der Bericht **ist morgen zu erstellen**.
: The report has to be prepared tomorrow.

Der Bericht **lässt sich morgen erstellen**.
: The report can be prepared tomorrow.

Der Bericht ist in einem Tag **machbar**.
: The report is feasible in one day.
:::

:::{note}
These forms are close in meaning but not fully interchangeable in every context. Choose based on tone: formal instruction, practical possibility, or concise quality judgment.
:::

## sein + zu + Infinitiv

This structure often appears in instructions, regulations, and formal writing. It usually means that something must or can be done, and it sounds more formal than many everyday alternatives.

> **sein** (conjugated) + zu + Infinitiv

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der Antrag **ist bis Ende Mai einzureichen**.
: The application must be submitted by the end of May.

Die Daten **sind vertraulich zu behandeln**.
: The data must be treated confidentially.

Diese Frage **ist leicht zu beantworten**.
: This question can be answered easily.

Alle Unterlagen **waren erneut zu prüfen**.
: All documents had to be checked again.
:::

:::{warning}
Do not confuse this with a regular adjective + infinitive pattern. In *sein + zu + Infinitiv*, the whole structure carries modal-passive meaning.
:::

---

## sich lassen + Infinitiv

This form highlights whether something is practically possible and often feels natural in spoken and written everyday German. It is a productive alternative to *kann ... werden* passive patterns.

> **sich lassen** (conjugated) + Infinitiv

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Das Problem **lässt sich** schnell **lösen**.
: The problem can be solved quickly.

Der Fehler **ließ sich** im Test sofort **reproduzieren**.
: The error could be reproduced immediately in the test.

Solche Prozesse **lassen sich** gut **automatisieren**.
: Such processes can be automated well.

Die neue Software **lässt sich** ohne großen Aufwand **installieren**.
: The new software can be installed without much effort.
:::

:::{tip}
A practical rewrite pattern is often: *kann ... werden* -> *lässt sich ...*.
:::

---

## sein + Adjektiv auf -bar/-lich

Adjectives like *machbar*, *lesbar*, *verständlich* or *nutzbar* package passive-like meaning into a compact quality statement. At B2, this is valuable for concise evaluations in professional and academic contexts.

| Adjective | Typical equivalent |
|---|---|
| machbar | kann gemacht werden |
| lesbar | kann gelesen werden |
| verständlich | kann verstanden werden |
| umsetzbar | kann umgesetzt werden |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der Zeitplan ist unter diesen Bedingungen **machbar**.
: The timeline is feasible under these conditions.

Die Handschrift ist kaum **lesbar**.
: The handwriting is hardly readable.

Die Argumentation war für alle **verständlich**.
: The argumentation was understandable for everyone.

Das Konzept ist im aktuellen Budget **umsetzbar**.
: The concept is implementable within the current budget.
:::

:::{note}
Not every verb forms a natural *-bar* adjective. Choose lexicalized, established forms and check whether the adjective sounds idiomatic in context.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note
- Core passive across tenses: **werden** + Partizip II, with tense shown mainly through **werden**.
- Passive perfect forms use **sein** + Partizip II + **worden**.
- Modal passive: modal verb + Partizip II + **werden**.
- Passive alternatives are central at B2: *sein + zu + Infinitiv*, *sich lassen + Infinitiv*, and adjective forms in *-bar/-lich*.
- Choose structure by register and communicative goal, not only by grammar equivalence.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution
- *Der Bericht ist geprüft geworden.* -> Der Bericht ist geprüft **worden**. (Use **worden** in passive Perfekt.)
- *Die Daten müssen analysiert.* -> Die Daten müssen analysiert **werden**. (Do not omit final **werden** in modal passive.)
- *Das Formular ist zu ausfüllen werden.* -> Das Formular ist **auszufüllen**. (Do not combine *sein + zu* with *werden*.)
- *Die Aufgabe lässt sich gelöst.* -> Die Aufgabe lässt sich **lösen**. (Use infinitive after *sich lassen*.)
- *Der Plan ist organisierbarlich.* -> Der Plan ist **organisierbar**. (Use established adjective forms only.)
:::
