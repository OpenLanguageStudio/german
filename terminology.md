# Terminology

Grammar has its own words, and German textbooks and teachers use them all the time. Each entry here gives one term in plain language, its German name in brackets, and one example. Articles in the course link to this page the first time they use a term.

## Parts of speech

```{glossary}
Noun : Nomen
  A word that names a person, place, thing or idea, and in German it always starts with a capital letter. *Example: der Hund, the dog.*

Pronoun : Pronomen
  A short word that stands in for a noun, so the noun need not be repeated. *Example: Anna ist müde. Sie schläft. (Anna is tired. She is sleeping.)*

Personal pronoun : Personalpronomen
  A word for a person or thing already known, such as I, you, she or they. *Example: ich, du, sie, wir.*

Reflexive pronoun : Reflexivpronomen
  A pronoun that points back to the subject of the sentence, like myself or herself in English. *Example: Ich wasche mich. (I wash myself.)*

Relative pronoun : Relativpronomen
  A word that opens a relative clause and refers back to the noun before it, like who or which in English. *Example: der Mann, der hier wohnt (the man who lives here).*

Verb : Verb
  A word for an action or a state, such as to run, to sleep or to be. *Example: Ich lerne Deutsch. (I am learning German.)*

Adjective : Adjektiv
  A word that describes a noun, saying what it is like. *Example: das große Haus, the big house.*

Adverb : Adverb
  A word that says how, when or where something happens, and it takes no endings in German. *Example: Sie singt schön. (She sings beautifully.)*

Preposition : Präposition
  A small word in front of a noun that shows place, time or direction, such as in, on or with. *Example: mit dem Bus, by bus.*

Two-way preposition : Wechselpräposition
  A preposition that takes the dative for a location and the accusative for a movement towards a place. *Example: Ich bin in der Stadt. Ich gehe in die Stadt.*

Conjunction : Konjunktion
  A word that joins words, phrases or clauses, such as and, but or because. *Example: Ich trinke Tee und du trinkst Kaffee.*

Question word : Fragewort
  A word that opens a question asking for information, such as who, what or where. *Example: Wo wohnst du? (Where do you live?)*

Compound noun : Kompositum
  A noun made by joining two or more words into one, where the last word sets the gender. *Example: das Haus + die Tür = die Haustür, the front door.*

Contraction : Kontraktion
  One short word made by joining a preposition and an article. *Example: zu dem becomes zum.*
```

## Articles

```{glossary}
Definite article : bestimmter Artikel
  The word for the in English, used for something specific; German has der, die and das. *Example: der Tisch, the table.*

Indefinite article : unbestimmter Artikel
  The word for a or an in English, used for something not yet specific; German has ein and eine. *Example: eine Tasse, a cup.*

Negative article : Negativartikel
  The word kein, which sits in front of a noun and means no or not a. *Example: Ich habe kein Auto. (I do not have a car.)*

Possessive article : Possessivartikel
  A word in front of a noun that says whose it is, such as my, your or our. *Example: mein Bruder, my brother.*

Demonstrative article : Demonstrativartikel
  A word in front of a noun that points to one particular thing, such as this or that. *Example: dieser Tisch, this table.*
```

## Gender and number

```{glossary}
Gender : Genus
  The grammatical group a German noun belongs to, which decides the form of its article and is often unrelated to the thing's meaning. *Example: der Löffel, die Gabel, das Messer.*

Masculine : maskulin
  One of the three genders, used with the article der. *Example: der Mann, the man.*

Feminine : feminin
  One of the three genders, used with the article die. *Example: die Frau, the woman.*

Neuter : neutral
  One of the three genders, used with the article das. *Example: das Kind, the child.*

Singular : Singular
  The form of a word for one person or thing. *Example: das Buch, the book.*

Plural : Plural
  The form of a word for more than one person or thing. *Example: die Bücher, the books.*

Umlaut : Umlaut
  The two dots on ä, ö or ü, which change the sound of a vowel. *Example: der Apfel, die Äpfel.*
```

## The cases

```{glossary}
Declension : Deklination
  The way articles, adjectives, pronouns and some nouns change form to show their job in a sentence. *Example: der Mann, den Mann, dem Mann.*

Nominative case : Nominativ
  The form for the subject of a sentence, the person or thing doing the action. *Example: Der Hund schläft. (The dog is sleeping.)*

Accusative case : Akkusativ
  The form for the direct object, the person or thing an action is done to. *Example: Ich sehe den Hund. (I see the dog.)*

Dative case : Dativ
  The form for the indirect object, the person something is given to or done for, and after prepositions such as mit. *Example: Ich helfe dem Mann. (I help the man.)*

Genitive case : Genitiv
  The form that shows who something belongs to, like 's or of in English. *Example: das Auto des Mannes, the man's car.*
```

## Verbs

```{glossary}
Infinitive : Infinitiv
  The basic form of a verb, the one a dictionary lists, which ends in -en or -n in German. *Example: gehen, to go.*

Conjugation : Konjugation
  The way a verb changes its form to match who is doing the action and when. *Example: ich gehe, du gehst, sie geht.*

Verb stem : Verbstamm
  The part of a verb left after removing -en or -n from the infinitive, which the endings attach to. *Example: lern- in lernen.*

Prefix : Präfix
  A short piece at the front of a word that changes its meaning. *Example: an- in ankommen, to arrive.*

Regular verb : regelmäßiges Verb
  A verb that keeps its stem and follows the usual pattern in every form. *Example: machen, ich mache, ich habe gemacht.*

Irregular verb : unregelmäßiges Verb
  A verb that changes in ways the usual pattern does not predict, so its forms are learned one by one. *Example: sein, ich bin, du bist.*

Strong verb : starkes Verb
  A verb that changes the vowel of its stem in the past, and its past participle usually ends in -en. *Example: trinken, getrunken.*

Weak verb : schwaches Verb
  A verb that keeps its stem in the past, and its past participle ends in -t. *Example: kaufen, gekauft.*

Modal verb : Modalverb
  A verb such as can, must or want that adds a meaning to a second verb, which stands at the end. *Example: Ich kann gut schwimmen. (I can swim well.)*

Separable verb : trennbares Verb
  A verb with a prefix that splits off and moves to the end of a simple sentence. *Example: aufstehen: Ich stehe um sieben auf.*

Reflexive verb : reflexives Verb
  A verb used with a reflexive pronoun, where the action points back to the subject. *Example: sich freuen: Ich freue mich.*

Auxiliary verb : Hilfsverb
  A helping verb that joins a main verb to form a tense; in German these are haben, sein and werden. *Example: Ich habe gegessen. (I have eaten.)*

Past participle : Partizip II
  The verb form used with haben or sein to talk about the past, and it usually starts with ge-. *Example: gemacht, done.*

Imperative : Imperativ
  The verb form for giving an order, an instruction or a request. *Example: Komm bitte! (Please come!)*

Passive voice : Passiv
  A sentence form that puts the thing affected first and says what happens to it, not who does it. *Example: Das Haus wird gebaut. (The house is being built.)*
```

## Tenses and moods

```{glossary}
Tense : Tempus
  The verb form that shows when something happens: now, before or later. *Example: ich gehe, ich bin gegangen.*

Present tense : Präsens
  The verb form for what happens now or regularly, and in German also for planned future events. *Example: Ich arbeite heute. (I am working today.)*

Past tense : Vergangenheit
  Any verb form for what happened before now; German mostly uses the Perfekt or the Präteritum. *Example: Ich war müde. (I was tired.)*

Future tense : Futur
  The verb form built with werden for what will happen. *Example: Ich werde arbeiten. (I will work.)*

Perfekt : perfect tense
  The usual spoken past tense in German, built from haben or sein and a past participle. *Example: Ich habe gearbeitet. (I worked.)*

Präteritum : simple past
  The one-word past tense, common in writing and with verbs such as sein and haben. *Example: Ich war zu Hause. (I was at home.)*

Plusquamperfekt : past perfect
  The tense for something that happened before another event in the past. *Example: Ich hatte gegessen. (I had eaten.)*

Konjunktiv I : reported speech form
  The verb form used mainly in news reports to pass on what someone else said. *Example: Er sagt, er sei krank. (He says he is ill.)*

Konjunktiv II : conditional form
  The verb form for polite requests, wishes and things that are not real. *Example: Ich hätte gern einen Kaffee. (I would like a coffee.)*
```

## Comparing

```{glossary}
Comparative : Komparativ
  The form of an adjective that compares two things, like bigger in English, usually made with -er. *Example: größer, bigger.*

Superlative : Superlativ
  The form of an adjective that marks the highest degree, like biggest in English. *Example: am größten, the biggest.*
```

## Sentences

```{glossary}
Subject : Subjekt
  The person or thing that does the action or is described in a sentence. *Example: Der Hund schläft. (The dog is sleeping.)*

Object : Objekt
  A person or thing in a sentence that is affected by the action of the verb. *Example: Ich lese ein Buch. (I am reading a book.)*

Direct object : Akkusativobjekt
  The person or thing an action is done to directly, answering whom or what. *Example: Ich kaufe den Tisch. (I am buying the table.)*

Indirect object : Dativobjekt
  The person who receives something or benefits from the action, answering to whom. *Example: Ich gebe dem Kind einen Apfel. (I give the child an apple.)*

Clause : Satz
  A group of words with its own subject and verb, which can be a whole sentence or part of one. *Example: Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.*

Main clause : Hauptsatz
  A clause that can stand alone as a full sentence. *Example: Ich bleibe zu Hause. (I am staying at home.)*

Subordinate clause : Nebensatz
  A clause that depends on a main clause, opens with a word such as weil, and puts its verb at the end. *Example: weil ich krank bin, because I am ill.*

Relative clause : Relativsatz
  A subordinate clause that gives more information about a noun, like who lives here in English. *Example: die Frau, die hier wohnt.*

Word order : Wortstellung
  The order of the words in a sentence, which in German follows fixed rules about where the verb goes. *Example: Heute gehe ich ins Kino.*

Sentence bracket : Satzklammer
  The pattern where two parts of a German verb sit around the rest of the sentence, one in second place and one at the end. *Example: Ich habe gestern Pizza gegessen.*

Negation : Negation
  The way of saying that something is not the case, in German mostly with nicht or kein. *Example: Ich komme nicht. (I am not coming.)*
```
