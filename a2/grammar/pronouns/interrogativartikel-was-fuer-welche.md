# Interrogativartikel: was für ein und welch-

Two German question words often confuse learners at this level: *was für ein* and *welch-*. They look similar and can both be translated as "what", but they ask very different things. The difference is what makes a question precise rather than vague.

---

## Was für ein(e)...? – What kind of?

Use *was für ein* when you want to know the **type or nature** of something. You have no particular set of options in mind, the question is open. It is the equivalent of "What kind of?" or "What sort of?".

The word *ein* declines just like the indefinite article – *ein, eine, einen*, etc. The noun after *was für ein* takes the case determined by its role in the sentence.

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | was für **ein** ... | was für **eine** ... | was für **ein** ... |
| Akk. | was für **einen** ... | was für **eine** ... | was für **ein** ... |
| Dat. | was für **einem** ... | was für **einer** ... | was für **einem** ... |

:::{note}
With **plural nouns** and **uncountable nouns** (things you cannot count), drop the *ein* entirely and use bare *was für*:

- Was für **Bücher** liest du? – What kind of books do you read?
- Was für **Musik** magst du? – What kind of music do you like?
:::

### Examples

:::{div} ol-examples
Was für **ein** {noun}`Auto` hast du?
: What kind of car do you have?

Was für **eine** {noun}`Wohnung` suchst du?
: What kind of apartment are you looking for?

Was für **einen** {noun}`Beruf` hat sie?
: What kind of job does she have?

Was für **ein** {noun}`Film` war das?
: What kind of film was that?

Was für **ein** {noun}`Buch` liest du gerade?
: What kind of book are you reading at the moment?

Was für **Sprachen** sprichst du?
: What kind of languages do you speak?
:::

---

## Welch-...? – Which one?

Use *welch-* when you are asking about a **specific choice from a known set**. There are particular options already in view, on a menu, in a shop, on a list, and you want to know which one is meant or preferred.

*Welch-* declines like the definite article (*der/die/das*). Match the ending to the gender and case of the noun.

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | **welcher** | **welche** | **welches** | **welche** |
| Dat. | **welchem** | **welcher** | **welchem** | **welchen** |
| Akk. | **welchen** | **welche** | **welches** | **welche** |

:::{tip}
A quick way to remember the endings: they follow the same pattern as *der, die, das* → *welcher, welche, welches*. If you know the definite article for a noun, you know the nominative form of *welch-*.
:::

### Examples

:::{div} ol-examples
**Welches** {noun}`Zimmer` ist frei?
: Which room is free?

**Welchen** {noun}`Zug` nimmst du?
: Which train are you taking?

**Welche** {noun}`Jacke` gefällt dir am besten?
: Which jacket do you like best?

Mit **welchem** {noun}`Bus` fährst du?
: Which bus are you taking?

**Welche** {noun}`Filme` habt ihr gesehen?
: Which films did you watch?

**Welchen** {noun}`Kaffee` möchtest du?
: Which coffee would you like?
:::

## The difference at a glance

The choice between the two question words depends on one thing: **do you have a specific set in mind?**

- **No specific set → was für ein** (open question about type)
- **Known set in view → welch-** (choosing from specific options)

| Situation | Question | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| You see someone with a car you don't recognise | Was für ein Auto ist das? | What kind of car is that? |
| You're at a car rental, choosing between three models | Welches Auto nimmst du? | Which car are you taking? |
| You're curious about someone's taste | Was für Musik hörst du? | What kind of music do you listen to? |
| You're holding up two concert tickets | Welches Konzert möchtest du? | Which concert would you like? |

:::{note}
In practice, the two words can sometimes overlap. Germans may occasionally use *welch-* where *was für ein* would be more natural, and vice versa. At A2 level, focus on the core contrast: **type/kind = was für ein**, **specific choice = welch-**.
:::

### More contrast examples

:::{div} ol-examples
Was für **einen** Tee möchtest du? (open, you haven't seen the options yet)
: What kind of tea would you like?

**Welchen** Tee möchtest du? (a menu or tea box is in front of you)
: Which tea would you like?

Was für **ein** Hotel habt ihr gebucht?
: What kind of hotel did you book?

**Welches** Hotel habt ihr gebucht? (choosing from a shortlist you both know)
: Which hotel did you book?
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- **was für ein(e)** asks about the **type or kind** of something, an open question with no specific set of options.
  - The *ein* declines like the indefinite article to match gender and case.
  - Plural and uncountable nouns: drop *ein*, just *was für* + noun.
- **welch-** asks about a **specific choice** from a known or visible set of options.
  - Declines like the definite article: *welcher / welche / welches / welche*.
- The *für* in *was für ein* does **not** fix the case of the noun, the noun's case comes from its grammatical role in the sentence.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *Was für \*welches Auto hast du?* → Use one or the other, not both: **Was für ein Auto hast du?**
- *Was für **ein** Bücher liest du?* → Plural nouns drop the *ein*: **Was für Bücher liest du?**
- *\*Welche Auto nimmst du?* (masculine noun, Akk) → **Welchen** Auto nimmst du?, match the ending to gender and case.
- *Was für **einen** Film läuft im Kino?* → The film is the subject (Nom): **Was für ein Film** läuft im Kino?
- Using *welch-* to ask about type when there are no visible options: prefer **was für ein** for open type questions.
:::
