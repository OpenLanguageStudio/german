# The zero article

The zero article means that German leaves the article out: *Ich bin Lehrerin*, *Ich trinke Kaffee*, *Wir wohnen in Berlin*. English does the same in many places, but not in all of them.

There is no separate word to learn, only a set of situations. Professions and nationalities after *sein* take no article. Languages, food and drink take none in general statements. {term}`Plural` {term}`nouns <noun>` take none where the {term}`singular` would have *ein*, and names of cities and most countries take none either. When a sentence means one specific item, the article comes back: *Der Kaffee ist kalt*.

## Professions and nationalities after *sein*

When you say what someone is by profession or nationality, German drops the article. English says "I am **a** teacher"; German says only *Ich bin Lehrerin*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich bin **Lehrerin**.
: I am a teacher.

Er ist **Student**.
: He is a student.

Sie ist **Italienerin**.
: She is Italian.
:::

:::{tip}
This also works with *werden* and *als*: *Sie wird Ärztin* (she is becoming a doctor). The pattern is the same: no article before the profession.
:::

## Languages, food, and drink

General statements about languages, food, drink, and other substances take no article. You talk about the thing in general, not one specific portion of it.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich spreche **Deutsch** und **Englisch**.
: I speak German and English.

Er trinkt gern **Tee**.
: He likes drinking tea.

Wir essen **Brot** mit **Käse**.
: We eat bread with cheese.
:::

When you mean one specific item, the article comes back: *Der Kaffee ist kalt* (the coffee in front of you).

## Plurals: the missing *ein*

*Ein* and *eine* have no plural form. Where English says "apples" or "some apples", German uses the bare plural noun.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Indefinite | ein Apfel | Äpfel |
| Indefinite | eine Frage | Fragen |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich kaufe **Äpfel**.
: I am buying apples.

Sie hat **Kinder**.
: She has children.

Wir brauchen **Eier**.
: We need eggs.
:::

## Cities and countries

Names of cities and most countries take no article: *in Berlin*, *nach Deutschland*, *aus Österreich*. A few country names do carry an article (*die Schweiz*, *die Türkei*); learn those as exceptions.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich wohne in **Berlin**.
: I live in Berlin.

Wir fahren nach **Deutschland**.
: We are going to Germany.

Kommst du aus **Österreich**?
: Do you come from Austria?
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin ein Lehrer.* → Ich bin **Lehrer**. (profession after *sein*, no article)
- *\*Ich spreche das Deutsch.* → Ich spreche **Deutsch**. (languages take no article)
- *\*Ich kaufe eine Äpfel.* → Ich kaufe **Äpfel**. (*ein* has no plural)
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- No article after *sein* for professions and nationalities: *Ich bin Lehrerin.*
- No article for languages, food, and drink in general statements: *Ich trinke Kaffee.*
- The plural of *ein* is nothing: *ein Apfel*, but *Äpfel*.
- Cities and most countries have no article: *in Berlin*, *nach Deutschland*.
:::
