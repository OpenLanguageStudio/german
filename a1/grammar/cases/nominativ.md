# The nominative case

The nominative is the case of the {term}`subject`: the person or thing doing the action, or the one the sentence is about. It is also the form that dictionaries list.

A case is the form a {term}`noun` and its article take to show their job in the sentence. Every noun starts out in the nominative, which is why *der*, *die* and *das* are the first forms learners meet. The other cases are defined by how they differ from it. German can open a sentence with something other than the subject, so the case marking, and not the position, shows who is acting.

## The subject of the sentence

The subject answers the question *wer?* (who?) or *was?* (what?). It usually stands at the front, but German {term}`word order` is flexible; the case marking, not the position, shows who is acting.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
{subj}`Der Mann` arbeitet in Hamburg.
: The man works in Hamburg.

{subj}`Die Kinder` spielen im Garten.
: The children are playing in the garden.

{subj}`Wer` kommt heute?
: Who is coming today?
:::

## After *sein* and *heißen*

With *sein* (to be) and *heißen* (to be called), both sides of the sentence stay in the nominative. Nothing is acted upon; the two halves describe the same person or thing.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Das ist {subj}`der Bahnhof`.
: That is the train station.

Das ist {subj}`eine gute Idee`.
: That is a good idea.

Er heißt {subj}`Peter`.
: His name is Peter.
:::

## The article forms

The nominative forms are the familiar ones from the article system:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Definite | der | die | das | die |
| Indefinite | ein | eine | ein | – |
| Negative | kein | keine | kein | keine |

{term}`Possessive articles <possessive article>` (*mein*, *dein*, ...) follow the *kein* pattern.

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Das ist den Bahnhof.* → Das ist **der** Bahnhof. (*sein* takes the nominative, never *den*)
- *\*Ein Frau wartet.* → **Eine** Frau wartet. ({term}`feminine` nominative is *eine*)
- Assuming the first noun is always the subject: German can start with time or place; check the article, not the position.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- The nominative marks the subject: who or what does the action.
- Ask *wer oder was?* to find the subject; word position alone is not reliable.
- After *sein* and *heißen*, both sides are nominative.
- The nominative article forms are the dictionary forms: *der, die, das*; *ein, eine, ein*.
:::
