# *du*, *ihr* or *Sie*

German has three forms of address where English has one *you*: *du*, *ihr* and *Sie*. Picking the right one is a social decision as much as a grammatical one.

*Du* is for one person a speaker knows well, and *ihr* is its {term}`plural`. *Sie* is the formal form, for one person or several, and it always has a capital letter. Each form takes its own {term}`verb` ending: *-st* with *du*, *-t* with *ihr* and *-en* with *Sie*. Other words change too, so *Wie geht es dir?* becomes *Wie geht es Ihnen?* with *Sie*.

## The three forms

| Form | Who | Verb form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| du | one person, informal | -st | Kommst du aus Berlin? |
| ihr | several people, informal | -t | Wo wohnt ihr? |
| Sie | one or several, formal | -en | Kommen Sie aus Berlin? |

*Sie* is always capitalised and always takes the *-en* form, whether you address one director or five.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Bist {pronoun}`du` müde?
: Are you tired? (one friend)

Seid {pronoun}`ihr` fertig?
: Are you ready? (several friends)

Sind {pronoun}`Sie` Frau Weber?
: Are you Mrs Weber? (formal)
:::

## Who gets which

*Du* is for friends, family, children, and usually fellow students. *Ihr* is simply the plural of *du*. *Sie* is for adults you do not know, officials, shop staff, and anyone at work until offered otherwise. The rule of thumb from introducing yourself still holds: when unsure, start with *Sie*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Wie geht es {dativ}`dir`?
: How are you? (du-person)

Wie geht es {dativ}`euch`?
: How are you? (ihr-people)

Wie geht es {dativ}`Ihnen`?
: How are you? (Sie-person)
:::

## Switching to *du*

The move from *Sie* to *du* is usually offered by the older or more senior person: *Wir können du sagen* (we can switch to du). Accepting is easy and pleasant; offering upward yourself is the thing to avoid.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Wir können du sagen.
: We can switch to du.

Sagen wir du?
: Shall we use du?
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Kommst Sie aus Berlin?* → **Kommen** Sie aus Berlin? (Sie takes the -en form)
- Using *du* with officials or shop staff: stay with **Sie** until offered.
- *\*Wie geht es du?* → Wie geht es **dir**? (the chunk uses the {term}`dative <dative case>` form)
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *du* = one informal person, *ihr* = several, *Sie* = formal, always capitalised.
- Verb endings: du -st, ihr -t, Sie -en.
- Default to *Sie* with adult strangers; wait for the offer to switch.
- The chunks differ too: *dir / euch / Ihnen* in *Wie geht es ...?*
:::
