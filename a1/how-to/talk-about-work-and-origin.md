# Talk about work and origin

Talking about profession and origin fills the second half of a typical first conversation. It covers what someone does for a living, where they work, and where they come from.

Both topics run on a few short patterns. Work uses *Ich bin*, *Ich arbeite als* or *Ich arbeite bei*. A job takes no article, so German has no word for *a* in *Ich bin Lehrerin*. Origin uses *aus* plus a country or a city. The question then comes back the other way with *Und du?* or *Und Sie?*, so one answer usually leads to the next.

## When to use it

- The natural follow-up questions after names are exchanged
- Job-related small talk at courses, work, or with neighbours
- Explaining your background as a newcomer

## Key phrases

### Work

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Was machst du beruflich? \| What do you do for a living?` | informal |
| {stack}`Was sind Sie von Beruf? \| What is your profession?` | formal |
| {stack}`Ich bin ... \| I am a ...` | job, no article |
| {stack}`Ich arbeite als ... \| I work as a ...` | job, no article |
| {stack}`Ich arbeite bei ... \| I work at ...` | company name |

### Origin

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Woher kommen Sie? \| Where are you from?` | formal |
| {stack}`Ich komme aus ... \| I come from ...` | country or city |

## Talking about work

Three patterns cover nearly every answer: *Ich bin* + job, *Ich arbeite als* + job, and *Ich arbeite bei* + employer. Jobs take no article, and most have a female form in *-in*: *Lehrer/Lehrerin*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich bin Ingenieur.
: I am an engineer.

Sie arbeitet bei einer Bank.
: She works at a bank.

Ich arbeite als Kellner.
: I work as a waiter.
:::

Not working right now also has simple answers: *Ich studiere* (I study), *Ich suche Arbeit* (I am looking for work), *Ich arbeite nicht*.

## Origin with *aus*

*Woher kommst du?* asks for origin; the answer uses *aus* plus a country or city. A few country names carry an article and keep it after *aus*: *aus der Schweiz, aus der Türkei*.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich komme aus Italien.
: I come from Italy.

Meine Familie kommt aus der Ukraine.
: My family comes from Ukraine.

Ich lebe seit zwei Jahren in Deutschland.
: I have been living in Germany for two years.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Was machst du beruflich? / Was sind Sie von Beruf?* opens the work topic.
- Answer with *Ich bin ..., Ich arbeite als ..., Ich arbeite bei ...*; no article before the job.
- *Woher?* asks origin, answered with *aus* + place.
- *seit* + time says how long you have been here: *seit zwei Jahren*.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin ein Kellner.* → Ich bin **Kellner**. (no article before professions)
- *\*Ich arbeite in Siemens.* → Ich arbeite **bei** Siemens. (employers take bei)
- *\*Ich komme von Italien.* → Ich komme **aus** Italien. (origin uses aus)
:::
