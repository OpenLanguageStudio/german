# Introduce yourself

Introducing yourself in German takes a handful of short sentences: a name, where someone comes from, where they live, and sometimes age and languages. The same few questions then go back the other way.

Who is listening decides the questions. Adults met for the first time, staff in offices and shops, and new colleagues get the formal *Sie*. Friends, family, children and other people in a course get the informal *du*. The sentences about oneself stay the same either way. [Choose between du and Sie](choose-between-du-and-sie.md) covers the choice in full.

## Key phrases

### Name, origin and home

A name comes with *Ich bin* or *Ich heiße*, and both are everyday German. Three more {term}`verbs <verb>` carry the rest: *kommen* (to come), *wohnen* (to live) and *sprechen* (to speak).

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Ich bin … \| I'm …` | the most common way to give a name |
| {stack}`Ich heiße … \| My name is …` | just as common |
| {stack}`Mein Name ist … \| My name is …` | {bdg-light}`formal`, rarer in speech |
| {stack}`Ich komme aus … \| I'm from …` | a country or a city |
| {stack}`Ich wohne in … \| I live in …` | a city or a district |
| {stack}`Ich bin … Jahre alt. \| I'm … years old.` | *Jahre alt* is often left out |
| {stack}`Ich spreche … \| I speak …` | languages |
| {stack}`Freut mich! \| Nice to meet you!` | the reply to any introduction |
| {stack}`Mich auch! \| Me too!` | the reply to *Freut mich* |

### Asking back

Each question starts with a question word, *wie* (how), *woher* (where from) or *wo* (where), and the verb comes next. [Question words for circumstances](/a1/grammar/question-words/fragewoerter-umstaende.md) has the full set, and [Yes/no questions and W-questions](/a1/grammar/sentence-structure/fragen.md) the word order. With *Sie* the verb ends in *-en*. With *du* it ends in *-st*, as in *Wie heißt du?* and *Woher kommst du?*

:::::{tab-set}

::::{tab-item} Formal (Sie)
:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: maria
Wie heißen Sie?
: What's your name?

Woher kommen Sie?
: Where are you from?

Wo wohnen Sie?
: Where do you live?

Wie alt sind Sie?
: How old are you?
:::
::::

::::{tab-item} Informal (du)
:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: emilia
Wie heißt du?
: What's your name?

Woher kommst du?
: Where are you from?

Wo wohnst du?
: Where do you live?

Wie alt bist du?
: How old are you?
:::
::::

:::::

*Und Sie?* or *Und du?* turns the last question around without repeating it.

## A formal introduction

With *Sie*, people often add their surname and are then called *Frau Haddad* or *Herr Kaya*. Here Maria Berger meets Lina Haddad for the first time.

:::{culture-note}
In formal settings Germans often give only their surname, *Ich heiße Haddad*, and shake hands at a first meeting. With *du*, the first name is enough.
:::

:::{audio-examples}
:track: a1/how-to/introduce-yourself
:mode: pairs
:presenters: maria, lina
:title: Sich vorstellen
:title-en: Introduce yourself
:tags: introductions, personal details, formal

Guten Abend!
: Good evening!
Guten Abend!
: Good evening!

Wie heißen Sie?
: What's your name?
Ich heiße Lina Haddad.
: My name is Lina Haddad.

Woher kommen Sie?
: Where are you from?
Ich komme aus Syrien, aus Damaskus.
: I'm from Syria, from Damascus.

Wo wohnen Sie?
: Where do you live?
Ich wohne in Berlin, in Neukölln.
: I live in Berlin, in Neukölln.

Wie alt sind Sie?
: How old are you?
Ich bin einundvierzig.
: I'm forty-one.

Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie?
: Which languages do you speak?
Ich spreche Arabisch, Englisch und Deutsch.
: I speak Arabic, English and German.

Freut mich, Frau Haddad.
: Nice to meet you, Ms Haddad.
Freut mich auch, Frau Berger.
: Nice to meet you too, Ms Berger.
:::

## An informal introduction

Between people of the same age, at a party or in a course, *du* comes straight away and a first name is all anyone gives. Here Julia meets Deniz.

:::{audio-examples}
:track: a1/how-to/introduce-yourself-informally
:mode: pairs
:presenters: emilia, deniz
:title: Sich vorstellen mit du
:title-en: Introduce yourself informally
:tags: introductions, personal details, informal

Hallo! Wie heißt du?
: Hi! What's your name?
Ich bin Deniz. Und du?
: I'm Deniz. And you?

Ich bin Julia. Freut mich!
: I'm Julia. Nice to meet you!
Mich auch!
: Me too!

Woher kommst du?
: Where are you from?
Ich komme aus Berlin.
: I'm from Berlin.

Und deine Familie?
: And your family?
Meine Familie kommt aus der Türkei.
: My family is from Turkey.

Wo wohnst du?
: Where do you live?
Ich wohne in Kreuzberg.
: I live in Kreuzberg.

Wie alt bist du?
: How old are you?
Ich bin zweiundzwanzig.
: I'm twenty-two.

Sprichst du Türkisch?
: Do you speak Turkish?
Ja, Türkisch und Deutsch.
: Yes, Turkish and German.

:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Ich bin …* and *Ich heiße …* both give a name. *Ich komme aus …*, *Ich wohne in …* and *Ich spreche …* give the rest.
- The questions start with *wie*, *woher* or *wo*, or simply *Und Sie? / Und du?*
- *Sie* is for adults met for the first time, *du* for friends and people in a course. The sentences about oneself don't change.
- *Freut mich* answers an introduction, and *Mich auch* answers *Freut mich*.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin von England.* → Ich komme **aus** England. (origin takes *aus*; learn more in [Local prepositions with dative](/a1/grammar/prepositions/lokale-praepositionen-dativ.md))
- *\*Wo kommst du?* → **Woher** kommst du? (origin asks "where from"; learn more in [Question words for circumstances](/a1/grammar/question-words/fragewoerter-umstaende.md))
- *\*Ich komme aus Türkei.* → Ich komme aus **der** Türkei. (a few countries take an article; learn more in [Countries and languages](/a1/vocabulary/countries-and-languages-1.md))
:::
