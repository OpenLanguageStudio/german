# Introduce yourself

Introducing yourself in German rests on four sentences: name, origin, where home is, and age. The same four questions come back the other way, in the formal *Sie* or the informal *du*.

German calls it *sich vorstellen*. Each of the four sentences starts with *ich* and a {term}`verb`: *Ich heiße*, *Ich komme aus*, *Ich wohne in* and *Ich bin*. The questions use the same verbs with a different ending, as in *Wie heißt du?* and *Woher kommst du?* Age is the one surprise, because German says it with *sein* (to be) and never with *haben* (to have). Turning a question around takes only *Und du?* or *Und Sie?*

:::{situations}
:::

## Key phrases

### Name, origin, home and age

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Ich heiße … \| My name is …` | the usual opener |
| {stack}`Mein Name ist … \| My name is …` | {bdg-light}`formal` |
| {stack}`Ich komme aus … \| I come from …` | a country or a city |
| {stack}`Ich wohne in … \| I live in …` | a city or a district |
| {stack}`Ich bin … Jahre alt. \| I am … years old.` | with a number |
| {stack}`Ich spreche … \| I speak …` | languages |

### Asking and replying

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Wie heißen Sie? / Wie heißt du? \| What is your name?` | formal / informal |
| {stack}`Woher kommen Sie? / Woher kommst du? \| Where are you from?` | formal / informal |
| {stack}`Freut mich! \| Nice to meet you!` | the reply to any introduction |
| {stack}`Mich auch. \| Me too.` | the reply to *Freut mich* |

## The four sentences

Three verbs do almost all the work: *heißen* (to be called), *kommen* (to come) and *wohnen* (to live). With *ich* they all end in **-e**. The fourth sentence, for age, uses *bin*, the *ich* form of *sein*.

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: lina
Ich heiße Lina.
: My name is Lina.

Ich komme aus Syrien.
: I come from Syria.

Ich wohne in Berlin, in Neukölln.
: I live in Berlin, in Neukölln.

Ich bin einundvierzig Jahre alt.
: I am forty-one years old.
:::

A few countries take an article, and then *aus* is followed by *der*: *aus der Türkei*, *aus der Ukraine*, *aus der Schweiz*. Most countries, such as *Syrien*, *Polen* or *Spanien*, take none.

## Asking the questions back

A conversation goes both ways. The {term}`question words <question word>` are *wie* (how or what), *woher* (where from) and *wo* (where). The verb ending changes with *Sie* or *du*, and a conversation keeps the form it starts with.

Woher kommst du?

:::{note}
Use **Sie** with adults you don't know, in offices and in shops. Use **du** with friends, family, children and other students in a course. When unsure, start with *Sie*.
:::

## Culture note

In formal settings Germans often give only their surname, *Ich heiße Weber*, and are then addressed as *Frau Weber* or *Herr Weber*. With *du*, the first name is enough. A handshake is usual at a first meeting. Age is a normal question in a language course, but adults rarely ask it of someone they have just met.

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Four sentences carry a full self-introduction: *Ich heiße … / Ich komme aus … / Ich wohne in … / Ich bin … Jahre alt.*
- The questions use *wie*, *woher* and *wo*, or simply *Und du? / Und Sie?*
- A conversation keeps *Sie* or *du* once it starts, and *Sie* is the safe start with strangers.
- *Freut mich* answers an introduction, and *Mich auch* answers *Freut mich*.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin heiße Anna.* → Ich **heiße** Anna. (*heißen* is the verb, so there is no *bin*)
- *\*Ich habe 25 Jahre alt.* → Ich **bin** 25 Jahre alt. (age uses *sein*, not *haben*)
- *\*Wo kommst du?* → **Woher** kommst du? (origin asks "where from")
- *\*Ich komme aus Türkei.* → Ich komme aus **der** Türkei. (a few countries take an article)
:::
