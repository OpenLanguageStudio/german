# Buy a ticket

Buying a train ticket in German starts with the destination: *Eine Fahrkarte nach Hamburg, bitte.* The questions that follow are about one way or return, the day, the price and how to pay.

At a counter the conversation keeps the same order almost every time. The person selling asks *Einfach oder hin und zurück?* (one way or return?) and then when. The price comes next, and the ticket last. Knowing the order makes each question easier to catch. Machines and apps ask the same questions on a screen.

## When to use it

- Buying return tickets at a station counter for a weekend away
- Reserving seats by the window for a long train journey
- Buying a day ticket for the buses and trains in a city
- Asking whether a child pays less

## Key phrases

### At the counter

*Fahrkarte* and *Ticket* are both everyday German, and *Fahrschein* appears on machines.

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Eine Fahrkarte nach …, bitte. \| A ticket to …, please.` | *nach* before a town: *nach Hamburg* |
| {stack}`einfach \| one way` | a single journey |
| {stack}`hin und zurück \| return` | there and back |
| {stack}`Was kostet das? \| How much is that?` | the price comes in euros: *hundertvierzig Euro* |
| {stack}`Kann ich mit Karte zahlen? \| Can I pay by card?` | *bar* is cash |
| {stack}`Ich möchte einen Platz reservieren. \| I'd like to reserve a seat.` | for long-distance trains; *Platz* is a seat |

*Ich möchte …* is the polite way to say what one wants. [*möchten*](/a1/grammar/verbs/moechten.md) covers it.

The person selling asks the same few questions back:

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: roman
Einfach oder hin und zurück?
: One way or return?

Wann möchten Sie fahren?
: When would you like to travel?

Gern. Am Fenster oder am Gang?
: Of course. By the window or on the aisle?
:::

### Tickets in town

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`ein Einzelfahrschein \| a single ticket` | one journey in one direction |
| {stack}`eine Tageskarte \| a day ticket` | for all buses and trains, all day |
| {stack}`das Deutschlandticket \| the Germany ticket` | a monthly ticket for local transport across the country |

## Buying a train ticket

At the counter, the customer gives the destination and the person selling asks the rest. Here a mother buys tickets for herself and her daughter.

:::{culture-note}
In many cities, a paper ticket bought in advance has to be stamped (*entwerten*) in a small box on the platform or in the bus. Tickets on a phone need no stamp. Inspectors check at random, and an unstamped ticket counts as no ticket at all.
:::

Guten Tag. Zwei Fahrkarten nach Hamburg, bitte.
: Hello. Two tickets to Hamburg, please.
Einfach oder hin und zurück?
: One way or return?

Hin und zurück, bitte.
: Return, please.
Wann möchten Sie fahren?
: When would you like to travel?

Am Samstag, am Morgen.
: On Saturday, in the morning.
Es gibt einen Zug um acht Uhr.
: There's a train at eight.

Meine Tochter ist sechzehn. Zahlt sie weniger?
: My daughter is sixteen. Does she pay less?
Nein, leider nicht. Sie zahlt den normalen Preis.
: No, I'm afraid not. She pays the full price.

Was kostet das?
: How much is that?
Zusammen hundertvierzig Euro.
: A hundred and forty euros together.

Ich möchte auch zwei Plätze reservieren.
: I'd also like to reserve two seats.
Gern. Am Fenster oder am Gang?
: Of course. By the window or on the aisle?

Am Fenster, bitte.
: By the window, please.
Gut. Hier sind Ihre Fahrkarten.
: Good. Here are your tickets.

Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?
: Can I pay by card?
Ja, natürlich.
: Yes, of course.

Und eine Tageskarte für Berlin?
: And a day ticket for Berlin?
Die gibt es am Automaten, dort drüben.
: You can get those from the machine over there.

Vielen Dank.
: Thank you very much.
Bitte schön. Gute Reise!
: You're welcome. Have a good trip!
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Eine Fahrkarte nach …, bitte* opens the purchase. *Ticket* works just as well.
- The first question back is *Einfach oder hin und zurück?* (one way or return?).
- *Was kostet das?* asks the price, and *Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?* the way to pay.
- In town, an *Einzelfahrschein* is one journey and a *Tageskarte* covers the whole day.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Eine Fahrkarte zu Hamburg.* → Eine Fahrkarte **nach** Hamburg. (towns and countries take *nach*; learn more in [Local prepositions with dative](/a1/grammar/prepositions/lokale-praepositionen-dativ.md))
- *\*Zwei Fahrkarte, bitte.* → Zwei **Fahrkarten**, bitte. (more than one needs the plural; learn more in [Noun gender and plurals](/a1/grammar/other/nomen-genus-plural.md))
:::
