# Make a phone call

Making a phone call in German starts with names. The person answering gives theirs, the caller says *Hier ist …*, and the call ends with *Auf Wiederhören* or a quick *Tschüss*.

On the phone there is no face to help. A few fixed phrases carry the call: who is speaking, who is wanted, and a number to call back. A bad line is normal, and *Ich höre Sie schlecht* (I can hardly hear you) is as useful as any other sentence here.

## When to use it

- Calling a doctor's practice and asking for the doctor
- Leaving a phone number for a call back
- Calling family to say that one will be late
- Answering the phone at work

## Key phrases

### Answering and giving a name

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Haddad, guten Tag. \| Haddad speaking, hello.` | at work or on a landline, often the surname alone |
| {stack}`Hier ist … \| This is …` | the caller's name, the usual way on the phone |
| {stack}`Ich bin's. \| It's me.` | {bdg-light}`informal`, to someone who knows the voice |
| {stack}`Was kann ich für Sie tun? \| What can I do for you?` | {bdg-light}`formal`, said by the person answering at work |
| {stack}`… ist gerade nicht da. \| … isn't here at the moment.` | the usual answer when someone is out |

### Calling an office

*Zurückrufen* (to call back) is a [separable verb](/a1/grammar/verbs/trennbare-verben.md): in a statement the *zurück* goes to the end: *Ich rufe Sie zurück.*

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: max
Guten Tag, hier ist Max Lehmann.
: Hello, this is Max Lehmann.

Kann ich bitte mit Frau Doktor Neumann sprechen?
: Could I speak to Dr Neumann, please?

Kann sie mich zurückrufen?
: Can she call me back?

Vielen Dank. Auf Wiederhören!
: Thank you very much. Goodbye!
:::

## A call to a practice

A call to an office uses *Sie* all the way through. Numbers are read in small groups, and names are often spelled, as in [Spell names and words aloud](spell-names-and-words-aloud.md). Here a caller rings a doctor's practice and asks for the doctor.

:::{culture-note}
*Auf Wiedersehen* means "until we see each other again". On the phone, many Germans say *Auf Wiederhören*, "until we hear each other again", in formal calls. Friends and family just say *Tschüss*.
:::

: Thank you very much. Goodbye!

## A call to family

A mobile shows who is calling, so family answer with a name and go straight to the point. A caller whose voice is known can say just *Ich bin's* (it's me). With *du*, *Ich höre dich schlecht* reports a bad line, and *Ich rufe dich zurück* promises a call back.

:::{audio-examples}
:track: a1/how-to/make-a-phone-call-family
:mode: pairs
:presenters: maria, emilia
:title: Telefonieren mit der Familie
:title-en: Call the family
:tags: phone calls, family, informal

Hallo, Julia!
: Hi, Julia!
Hallo, Mama! Hast du kurz Zeit?
: Hi, Mum! Have you got a minute?

Ja, klar. Was gibt's?
: Yes, sure. What's up?
Ich komme am Sonntag ein bisschen später.
: I'm coming a bit later on Sunday.

Kein Problem. Und Lukas?
: No problem. And Lukas?
Lukas kommt auch, mit seiner Wäsche.
: Lukas is coming too, with his laundry.

Natürlich, wie immer!
: Of course, as always!
Machst du einen Apfelkuchen?
: Are you making an apple cake?

Ja, gern. Wo bist du gerade?
: Yes, happily. Where are you right now?
Ich bin im Zug.
: I'm on the train.

Ich höre dich ganz schlecht.
: I can hardly hear you.
Ich rufe dich später zurück, okay?
: I'll call you back later, okay?

Gut. Bis später!
: All right. Talk later!
Tschüss, Mama!
: Bye, Mum!
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- The person answering gives a name. The caller says *Hier ist …*, or *Ich bin's* to family.
- *Kann ich bitte mit … sprechen?* asks for someone, and *… ist gerade nicht da* is the usual answer when they are out.
- *Können Sie mich zurückrufen?* asks for a call back. Numbers come in small groups.
- Formal calls end with *Auf Wiederhören*, family calls with *Tschüss* or *Bis später*.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Kann ich zu Frau Neumann sprechen?* → Kann ich **mit** Frau Neumann sprechen? (English "speak to" is *sprechen mit*; learn more in [The preposition mit](/a1/grammar/prepositions/mit-dativ.md))
- *\*Ich telefoniere dich morgen.* → Ich **rufe** dich morgen **an**. (*anrufen* takes the person directly; learn more in [Separable verbs](/a1/grammar/verbs/trennbare-verben.md))
:::
