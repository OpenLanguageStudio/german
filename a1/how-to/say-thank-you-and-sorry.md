# Say thank you and sorry

Thank you in German is *Danke*, and sorry in German is *Entschuldigung* or *Tut mir leid*. Each one has a short reply that comes back almost automatically.

Two of these words do two jobs. *Bitte* means "please", and it is also the usual reply to *Danke*, the German "you're welcome". *Entschuldigung* says sorry for something small, and it also opens a question to a stranger. *Tut mir leid* is for real regret.

## When to use it

- Thanking a shop assistant who found the right size
- Thanking a friend for a present
- Bumping into someone on a crowded U-Bahn
- Arriving late to meet a friend
- Hearing that a friend is ill

## Key phrases

### Thanking someone

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Danke! \| Thanks!` | the everyday thank-you, with anyone |
| {stack}`Danke schön! / Vielen Dank! \| Thank you very much!` | a little warmer; *Vielen Dank* also ends many emails |
| {stack}`Danke für … \| Thanks for …` | with a noun: *Danke für die Hilfe* (thanks for the help) |
| {stack}`Nein, danke. \| No, thanks.` | turns down an offer; *Danke* alone can mean the same |

### Replying to thanks

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Bitte! / Bitte schön! \| You're welcome!` | the same word as "please" |
| {stack}`Gern geschehen! \| My pleasure!` | often shortened to *Gern!* |
| {stack}`Kein Problem! \| No problem!` | very common in speech, with *du* and *Sie* |

### Saying sorry

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Entschuldigung! \| Sorry! / Excuse me!` | small things, and getting someone's attention |
| {stack}`Entschuldigen Sie! \| Excuse me!` | {bdg-light}`formal`; with *du*: *Entschuldige!* |
| {stack}`Tut mir leid. \| I'm sorry.` | real regret, and sympathy for bad news; also *Es tut mir leid* |
| {stack}`Sorry! \| Sorry!` | {bdg-light}`colloquial`, common among younger people |

### Replying to sorry

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Kein Problem. \| No problem.` | the most common reply |
| {stack}`Macht nichts. \| Never mind.` | short for *Das macht nichts* (that doesn't matter) |
| {stack}`Alles gut. \| It's all fine.` | {bdg-light}`colloquial`, very common in speech |

## Thanking and replying

*Danke* gets *Bitte* back, and a bigger thank-you often gets a bigger reply, such as *Gern geschehen*. To accept an offer, the answer is *Ja, gern* or *Ja, bitte*. *Danke* on its own, said to an offer, often means "no, thanks".

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: roman
Nein, danke.
: No, thanks.

Ja, gern!
: Yes, please!
:::

## Apologising and replying

*Entschuldigung* and *Sorry* cover small things, such as a late arrival or a step on someone's foot. *Tut mir leid* also answers bad news, much like English "I'm sorry to hear that". To stop a stranger and ask something, *Entschuldigung* comes first, as [Make and answer requests](make-and-answer-requests.md) shows.

:::{audio-examples}

:tags: politeness, informal

Entschuldigung!
: Sorry!
Kein Problem!
: No problem!

Sorry, ich bin zu spät!
: Sorry, I'm late!
Macht nichts.
: Never mind.

Tut mir leid!
: I'm sorry!
Alles gut.
: It's all fine.

Entschuldige, heute habe ich keine Zeit.
: Sorry, I don't have time today.
Schade! Dann am Sonntag?
: Too bad! Sunday, then?

Es tut mir wirklich leid.
: I'm really sorry.
Schon gut.
: It's fine.

Mein Laptop ist kaputt.
: My laptop is broken.
Oh, das tut mir leid!
: Oh, I'm sorry!

Ich bin krank.
: I'm ill.
Das tut mir leid! Gute Besserung!
: I'm sorry! Get well soon!

Entschuldigung, ist das dein Kaffee?
: Excuse me, is that your coffee?
Ja, danke!
: Yes, thanks!
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Danke*, *Danke schön* and *Vielen Dank* thank. *Bitte*, *Gern geschehen* and *Kein Problem* reply.
- *Danke* alone, to an offer, can mean no. *Ja, gern* and *Ja, bitte* accept.
- *Entschuldigung* is for small things and for getting attention. *Tut mir leid* is for regret and bad news.
- *Kein Problem*, *Macht nichts* and *Alles gut* answer an apology.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Willkommen!* as the reply to *Danke* → **Bitte!** or **Gern geschehen!** (*Willkommen* only welcomes someone to a place)
- *Danke.* to accept a coffee → **Ja, gern.** or **Ja, bitte.** (*Danke* alone usually turns the offer down)
:::
