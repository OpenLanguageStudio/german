# Talk about family

Talking about family in German needs a few words for people and two verbs, *haben* and *sein*. The usual questions are who is in the family, what they are called, how old they are and where they live.

## When to use it

- Showing family photos to a colleague over lunch
- Answering a neighbour who asks about the children
- Meeting a partner's parents for the first time
- Telling a language course about home

## Key phrases

### Family words

Most family words work as in English. The ones below need a note. [Family](/a1/vocabulary/family-1.md) has the full set, with grandparents, aunts and cousins.

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`die Eltern \| parents` | plural only: *Meine Eltern wohnen in Köln.* |
| {stack}`die Geschwister \| brothers and sisters` | far more common than English *siblings* |
| {stack}`mein Mann, meine Frau \| my husband, my wife` | *Mann* and *Frau* also mean man and woman |
| {stack}`mein Freund, meine Freundin \| my boyfriend, my girlfriend` | also a friend: context decides |
| {stack}`das Einzelkind \| only child` | *Sie ist Einzelkind.* |

### Saying who is in the family

*Ich habe einen Bruder* (I have a brother) uses *haben*, and *ein* becomes *einen* before a *der* word such as *Bruder*. [The accusative](/a1/grammar/cases/akkusativ.md) explains why. *Kein* says there is nobody: *Ich habe keine Kinder*.

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Ich habe einen Bruder. \| I have a brother.` | *einen* for a *der* word |
| {stack}`Ich habe eine Tochter. \| I have a daughter.` | *eine* for a *die* word |
| {stack}`Ich habe zwei Kinder. \| I have two children.` | a number needs no article |
| {stack}`Ich habe keine Geschwister. \| I don't have any brothers or sisters.` | *keine*, never *nicht* |
| {stack}`Ich bin verheiratet. \| I'm married.` | *ledig* (single) is mostly for forms |
| {stack}`Ich bin Single. \| I'm single.` | the everyday word |

### Talking about each person

*Mein* or *meine* (my) picks out a person, and the sentence goes on as usual: *Mein Bruder heißt Lukas*, *Meine Mutter wohnt in Schöneberg*. *Mein* goes with *der* and *das* words, *meine* with *die* words and plurals, as [Possessive articles](/a1/grammar/articles/possessivartikel.md) shows.

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: lina
Ja, mein Mann heißt Tobias.
: Yes, my husband is called Tobias.

Meine Eltern wohnen in Damaskus.
: My parents live in Damascus.
:::

## A conversation about family

Between friends the questions use *du*: *Hast du Kinder?*, *Bist du verheiratet?*, *Hast du Geschwister?* With *Sie* the verb changes, as in *Haben Sie Kinder?* and *Sind Sie verheiratet?* *Meine Familie* (my family) covers everyone at once.

:::{audio-examples}
:track: a1/how-to/talk-about-family
:mode: pairs
:presenters: emilia, lina
:title: Über die Familie sprechen
:title-en: Talk about family
:tags: family, possessive article, informal

Hast du Kinder?
: Do you have children?
Ja, ich habe eine Tochter.
: Yes, I have a daughter.

Wie heißt sie?
: What's her name?
Sie heißt Lana.
: Her name is Lana.

Wie alt ist Lana?
: How old is Lana?
Sie ist sechzehn.
: She's sixteen.

Hat Lana Geschwister?
: Does Lana have brothers or sisters?
Nein, sie ist Einzelkind.
: No, she's an only child.

Bist du verheiratet?
: Are you married?
Ja, mein Mann heißt Tobias.
: Yes, my husband is called Tobias.

Wo wohnen deine Eltern?
: Where do your parents live?
Meine Eltern wohnen in Damaskus.
: My parents live in Damascus.

Ich habe einen Bruder.
: I have a brother.
Wie alt ist dein Bruder?
: How old is your brother?

Er ist fünfundzwanzig.
: He's twenty-five.
Wohnt er auch in Berlin?
: Does he live in Berlin too?

Ja, in Moabit. Er ist Student.
: Yes, in Moabit. He's a student.
Und deine Mutter?
: And your mother?

Meine Mutter wohnt in Schöneberg.
: My mother lives in Schöneberg.
Siehst du sie oft?
: Do you see her often?

Ja, am Sonntag essen wir immer bei meiner Mutter.
: Yes, on Sundays we always eat at my mother's.
Das ist schön!
: That's lovely!
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Ich habe einen Bruder / eine Tochter / zwei Kinder* says who is in the family, and *keine* says nobody.
- *Mein* goes with *der* and *das* words, *meine* with *die* words and plurals: *mein Bruder*, *meine Mutter*, *meine Eltern*.
- *Hast du Geschwister?*, *Hast du Kinder?* and *Bist du verheiratet?* are the usual questions.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich habe ein Bruder.* → Ich habe **einen** Bruder. (learn more in [The accusative](/a1/grammar/cases/akkusativ.md))
- *\*Ich habe nicht Kinder.* → Ich habe **keine** Kinder. (learn more in [The negative article](/a1/grammar/articles/negativartikel.md))
- *\*Meine Bruder heißt Lukas.* → **Mein** Bruder heißt Lukas. (learn more in [Possessive articles](/a1/grammar/articles/possessivartikel.md))
:::
