# Choose between du and Sie

*Du* is the informal you in German, for friends, family and children. *Sie* is the formal you in German, for adults met for the first time, staff and officials.

Du or Sie is a choice about the relationship, and it holds until someone changes it. With a new adult, *Sie* is the safe start. Once both sides agree, they switch to *du* and rarely go back. Saying *du* is called *duzen*, and saying *Sie* is *siezen*. A learner who mixes them up is usually forgiven.

## When to use it

- Meeting a new colleague on the first day at work
- Asking a shop assistant where something is
- Talking to the teenage child of a neighbour
- Joining an evening course with other adult learners
- Being offered *du* by a colleague after a few weeks

## Key phrases

### The same questions both ways

Most questions change in two places: the verb and the word for "you". *Dir* and *dein* also become *Ihnen* and *Ihr*.

### Offering du

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Wollen wir uns duzen? \| Shall we say du?` | the usual offer, often from the older or more senior person |
| {stack}`Sag ruhig du. \| Feel free to say du.` | said to someone who is still saying *Sie* |
| {stack}`Ja, gern! \| Yes, gladly!` | the usual answer; a no is rare and sounds cold |

## How the verb changes

With *du*, the verb ends in *-st*: *du kommst*. With *Sie*, it ends in *-en*, the same form as for *wir* (we): *Sie kommen*. *Sie* always has a capital S. [Regular verbs in the present](/a1/grammar/verbs/regelmassige-verben.md) has all the endings, and [du, ihr or Sie](/a1/grammar/pronouns/anredeformen.md) has the {term}`pronouns <pronoun>`, including *ihr* for several friends.

## Switching to du

The usual offer is *Wollen wir uns duzen?* (shall we say *du*?). Once *du* is agreed, first names replace *Herr* and *Frau*, and every verb changes with it. Here Maria, a teacher, offers *du* to Lina, who was once in her evening course.

:::{culture-note}
Some workplaces, such as many Berlin start-ups, use *du* with everyone from the first day. In banks, offices and with older people, *Sie* is still the norm. Among students and young people, *du* comes straight away.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Du* is for friends, family, children and young people. *Sie* is for other adults until *du* is offered.
- With *du* the verb ends in *-st*. With *Sie* it ends in *-en*, and *Sie* has a capital S.
- *Dir* and *dein* go with *du*. *Ihnen* and *Ihr* go with *Sie*.
- *Wollen wir uns duzen?* offers *du*, and *Ja, gern!* accepts it.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Woher kommst Sie?* → Woher **kommen** Sie? (*Sie* takes the *-en* form; learn more in [Regular verbs in the present](/a1/grammar/verbs/regelmassige-verben.md))
- *\*Wie heißen sie?* in a message to a stranger → Wie heißen **Sie**? (small *sie* means "she" or "they"; learn more in [du, ihr or Sie](/a1/grammar/pronouns/anredeformen.md))
- *Du* to a shop assistant or an official → **Sie**, until *du* is offered.
:::
