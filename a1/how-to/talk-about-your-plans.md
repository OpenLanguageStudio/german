# Talk about your plans

Talking about plans in German needs no new tense at first. The present tense and a time word carry them: *Morgen arbeite ich*, *Am Sonntag esse ich bei meiner Mutter*.

A plan is either fixed or wanted. A fixed plan, with a day or a time attached, is the present tense, the same form used for every day. A plan someone wants but hasn't fixed yet takes *wollen*: *Ich will ein Buch schreiben*. Either way, the verb comes second.

## When to use it

- Telling a colleague about the weekend on Friday afternoon
- Answering a friend's *Was machst du heute Abend?*
- Talking about holiday plans for the summer
- Saying that a plan isn't fixed yet

## Key phrases

### Asking about plans

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Was machst du am Wochenende? \| What are you doing at the weekend?` | *Was machen Sie …?* with *Sie* |
| {stack}`Hast du schon Pläne? \| Do you have any plans yet?` | *schon* here means yet |
| {stack}`Was machst du heute Abend? \| What are you doing tonight?` | *heute Abend* is this evening |

### Saying what's planned

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Am Samstag arbeite ich. \| I'm working on Saturday.` | time word first, verb second |
| {stack}`Ich will … \| I want to …` | the second verb goes to the end |
| {stack}`Vielleicht … \| Maybe …` | for a plan that isn't fixed |
| {stack}`Ich weiß noch nicht. \| I don't know yet.` | *noch nicht* means not yet |

## Plans with a day or a time

English often puts a plan in the present: *I'm working tomorrow*. German does the same with its one present form: *Ich arbeite morgen*. The future in German has its own form with *werden*, but a plan with a day attached rarely needs it.

When the time word comes first, the verb stays in second place: *Morgen arbeite ich*. [The verb in second position](/a1/grammar/sentence-structure/verb-position-2.md) explains the order.

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: emilia
Heute Abend schreibe ich einen Artikel.
: Tonight I'm writing an article.

Am Sonntag gehe ich auf den Flohmarkt.
: On Sunday I'm going to the flea market.

Ich weiß noch nicht. Vielleicht lese ich.
: I don't know yet. Maybe I'll read.
:::

## Wanted plans with *wollen*

A plan someone wants takes *wollen* (to want), one of the [modal verbs](/a1/grammar/verbs/koennen-muessen-wollen.md). *Will* stands second and the main verb goes to the end: *Ich will ein Café aufmachen*.

*Ich will* is fine for one's own plans. For asking for something, the softer *Ich möchte* is the usual choice, and [*möchten*](/a1/grammar/verbs/moechten.md) covers it.

:::{ol-examples}
:presenters: deniz
Ich will ein eigenes Café aufmachen.
: I want to open my own café.

Am Samstag will ich mit Max zeichnen.
: On Saturday I want to draw with Max.

Meine Schwester Elif will Anwältin werden.
: My sister Elif wants to become a lawyer.
:::

: After work I want to go skateboarding.

: On Saturday I want to draw with Max.

Ich möchte mehr zeichnen.
: I'd like to draw more.

: On Sunday I'm visiting my family.

Meine Schwester Elif will Anwältin werden.
: My sister Elif wants to become a lawyer.

Heute Abend will ich nichts machen.
: Tonight I don't want to do anything.

Vielleicht gehe ich auf das Tempelhofer Feld.
: Maybe I'll go to Tempelhofer Feld.

Was willst du am Wochenende machen?
: What do you want to do at the weekend?

Hast du schon Pläne?
: Do you have any plans yet?
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- A fixed plan is the present tense with a time word: *Morgen arbeite ich*.
- After a time word at the start, the verb still comes second.
- A wanted plan takes *wollen*, with the main verb at the end: *Ich will ein Buch schreiben*.
- *Vielleicht* and *Ich weiß noch nicht* answer for a plan that isn't fixed.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Morgen ich arbeite.* → Morgen **arbeite ich**. (learn more in [The verb in second position](/a1/grammar/sentence-structure/verb-position-2.md))
- *\*Ich will morgen arbeiten* for "I will work tomorrow" → Ich **arbeite** morgen. (*will* means "want")
- *\*Ich will gehen ins Kino.* → Ich will ins Kino **gehen**. (learn more in [Modal verbs](/a1/grammar/verbs/koennen-muessen-wollen.md))
:::
