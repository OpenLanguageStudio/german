# Say how you feel

*Wie geht's?* is how are you in German, and an honest answer often goes beyond *gut*. Moods, tiredness, illness and pain each have a short pattern of their own.

English uses *I am* for almost every feeling: *I'm tired*, *I'm hungry*, *I'm cold*. German splits them. A mood takes *sein*, as in English. Hunger, fever and pain take *haben*: *Ich habe Hunger*. Feeling cold or warm takes *mir*: *Mir ist kalt*.

## When to use it

- Answering a colleague's *Wie geht's?* on a Monday morning
- Checking on a friend who looks tired
- Phoning work to say someone is ill
- Describing symptoms at a doctor's reception

## Key phrases

### Asking how someone is

*Wie geht es Ihnen?* is the formal question and *Wie geht's?* the everyday one. *Was ist los?* (What's wrong?) follows a bad answer.

::::{tab-item} Formal (Sie)

### Answering

*Mir geht es gut* (I'm fine) is the standard answer. *Mir* is the {term}`dative <dative case>` of *ich*, and [Personal pronouns: dative](/a1/grammar/pronouns/personalpronomen-dativ.md) explains it.

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Mir geht es gut. \| I'm fine.` | *Mir geht's gut* in speech |
| {stack}`Ganz gut. \| Pretty good.` | a little less than *gut* |
| {stack}`Es geht. \| So-so.` | neither good nor bad |
| {stack}`Nicht so gut. \| Not so good.` | the usual way to say *bad* |

### Moods, illness and pain

A body part that hurts takes *tut weh* (hurts). [The human body](/a1/vocabulary/the-human-body-1.md) has the words for body parts.

| Phrase | Notes |
|---|---|
| {stack}`Ich bin müde. \| I'm tired.` | *sein* with a mood: *nervös*, *glücklich*, *traurig* |
| {stack}`Ich bin krank. \| I'm ill.` | *Ich bin erkältet* for a cold |
| {stack}`Ich habe Kopfschmerzen. \| I have a headache.` | always plural: *-schmerzen* |
| {stack}`Mein Kopf tut weh. \| My head hurts.` | *tun weh* for more than one: *Meine Füße tun weh.* |
| {stack}`Ich habe Fieber. \| I have a temperature.` | *haben*, no article |
| {stack}`Ich habe Hunger. \| I'm hungry.` | *haben*, where English says *I'm* |
| {stack}`Mir ist kalt. \| I'm cold.` | *mir*, where English says *I'm* |
| {stack}`Gute Besserung! \| Get well soon!` | the reply to anyone who is ill |

## Between friends

Friends ask *Wie geht's?* every day, and a short honest answer is normal: *Nicht so gut*, *Ich bin müde*. The other person then asks *Was ist los?*

:::{audio-examples}
:track: a1/how-to/say-how-you-feel
:mode: pairs
:presenters: deniz, max
:title: Wie geht's?
:title-en: How are you?
:tags: feelings, informal

Morgen, Max!
: Morning, Max!
Morgen, Deniz!
: Morning, Deniz!

Wie geht's?
: How are you?
Nicht so gut.
: Not so good.

Was ist los?
: What's wrong?
Ich bin total müde.
: I'm really tired.

Hast du Kopfschmerzen?
: Do you have a headache?
Ja, ein bisschen.
: Yes, a bit.

Bist du krank?
: Are you ill?
Nein, nur müde.
: No, just tired.

Und sonst?
: And apart from that?
Ich bin ein bisschen nervös.
: I'm a bit nervous.

Warum?
: Why?
Heute kommt ein großer Kunde.
: A big client is coming today.

Mir geht's heute super.
: I'm doing great today.
Schön! Warum?
: Nice! Why?

Die Sonne scheint, und später fahre ich Skateboard.
: The sun's out, and later I'm going skateboarding.
Du hast es gut!
: Lucky you!

Hier, dein Kaffee. Viel Glück heute!
: Here's your coffee. Good luck today!
Danke!
: Thanks!
:::

## At the doctor's

At the doctor's, staff use *Sie* and the surname. The questions are about pain, fever and how long: *Seit wann?* (Since when?) is answered with *seit*, as in *Seit zwei Tagen*.

:::{culture-note}
Workers in Germany who are ill usually need a doctor's note, *die Krankschreibung*, from the fourth day off. Some employers ask for one from the first day.
:::

:::{audio-examples}

:mode: pairs
:presenters: lina, roman
:title: Wie geht es Ihnen?
:title-en: How are you? (formal)
:tags: feelings, the body, formal

Guten Morgen, Herr Petrenko!
: Good morning, Mr Petrenko!
Guten Morgen!
: Good morning!

Wie geht es Ihnen?
: How are you?
Nicht so gut, leider.
: Not so good, I'm afraid.

Was haben Sie denn?
: What's the matter?
Ich habe Halsschmerzen.
: I have a sore throat.

Haben Sie auch Fieber?
: Do you have a temperature too?
Ja, ein bisschen.
: Yes, a bit.

Tut der Kopf auch weh?
: Does your head hurt too?
Ja, mein Kopf tut sehr weh.
: Yes, my head hurts a lot.

Seit wann sind Sie krank?
: How long have you been ill?
Seit zwei Tagen.
: For two days.

Sind Sie müde?
: Are you tired?
Ja, ich bin sehr müde.
: Yes, I'm very tired.

Die Ärztin kommt gleich.
: The doctor will be with you soon.
Danke.
: Thank you.

Gute Besserung, Herr Petrenko!
: Get well soon, Mr Petrenko!
Vielen Dank.
: Thank you very much.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Wie geht es Ihnen?* and *Wie geht's?* ask, and *Mir geht es gut* or *Nicht so gut* answers.
- A mood takes *sein*: *Ich bin müde.* Hunger, fever and pain take *haben*: *Ich habe Kopfschmerzen.*
- A body part takes *tut weh*: *Mein Kopf tut weh.*
- *Mir ist kalt* says *I'm cold*, and *Gute Besserung!* answers anyone who is ill.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin gut.*, meaning *I'm fine* → **Mir geht es gut.** (*Ich bin gut* means *I'm good at it*)
- *\*Ich bin kalt.* → **Mir ist kalt.** (*Ich bin kalt* means *I'm a cold person*)
- *\*Wie geht es du?* → Wie geht es **dir**? (*dir* goes with *mir*; learn more in [Personal pronouns: dative](/a1/grammar/pronouns/personalpronomen-dativ.md))
:::
